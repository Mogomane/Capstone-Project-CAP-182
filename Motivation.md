# PART A: MOTIVATION

## Motivation for the Study

STADIOalot, through its operation within the e-commerce/logistics space, provides service to over **4.1 million active customers** and processes almost **58 million orders per year**. As the business grows in terms of scale, it continues to struggle to meet the goal of achieving profitability. The group’s operating profit margin is currently just **1.9%**. The potential for small, unnecessary costs related to specific orders to add up and become large-scale costs at such high volume makes operational efficiency and the cost associated with each parcel critical to protect profitability.

Returns are one of the most challenging problems that e-commerce businesses face today due to the operational, financial and logistical challenges that come with handling returns. Studies suggest that returns have become a rapidly increasing problem for many types of businesses and that there are both financial and operational costs involved in managing the process of returns (Duong et al., 2022; Frei et al., 2020). In addition, studies indicate that returns are a key area of concern for businesses, as uncertainty surrounding customer behaviour, product attributes and product quality make it difficult for businesses to develop effective strategies for managing returns (Ambilkar et al., 2022).

Returns pose a serious problem for fashion e-commerce companies because they can affect profitability (Hjort & Lantz, 2016). The problem posed by returns is particularly acute for STADIOalot because although the overall return on orders has risen from **11% to 15%**, the return rate for clothing/shoes has dramatically increased from **22% to 29%**. According to the briefing pack, while some products/sellers/size-and-colour combinations/customer purchasing behaviours result in higher return rates than others, STADIOalot currently does not identify which ones are associated with higher return rates until after the customer places their order.

Previous studies indicate that customer behaviour and buying behaviour can provide valuable insights into understanding why customers return merchandise. For example, Zhang et al. (2022) identified correlations between consumer online information-searching behaviours and product returns. Similarly, Minnema et al. (2016) determined that the type of online customer review could affect the decision to return a purchase. Based upon their results, it appears that both customer behaviour and product-related information contained within those behaviours contain signals about the likelihood of returning a product. This is relevant to STADIOalot as they collect clickstream data, search data, transaction data, product data, and returns data.

With all the above-mentioned types of data available, there exists a possibility for STADIOalot to transition from a reactive Return Management process to a proactive Return Management process based upon analysis of the collected data. As previously stated, various researchers have documented how Machine Learning methodologies can be used to forecast the volume of product returns and the contributing factors behind product returns (Cui et al., 2020; Mishra & Dutta, 2024). Furthermore, Karl (2025) states that the existence of substantial amounts of customer data and advancements in machine learning present new possibilities for predicting e-commerce returns. Even more recently, Cao et al. (2026) demonstrated the feasibility of utilizing a graph-based machine learning methodology to predict product return behaviour prior to payment, which highlights the ability to utilize predictive models to enable early and proactive intervention to prevent returns.

As such, the proposed project aligns well with STADIOalot's **2030 Strategic Priority to "break the returns cycle."** Specifically, the company desires to understand what drives returns and take actions to reduce returns that are avoidable before an order is fulfilled. Additionally, since STADIOalot has had multiple years of historical data regarding orders/transactions/products/customer behaviour/returns, there exists sufficient data to investigate the patterns that contribute to orders that result in a return.

Furthermore, previous research indicates that utilisation of information technology can be beneficial to improving returns management processes. Ahsan and Rahman (2022) identify significant areas of future research concerning e-tail product returns. Conversely, Ambilkar et al. (2022) emphasise the significance of enhancing informational transparency in improving returns management practices. Therefore, the prior research presents a robust foundation for investigating whether STADIOalot can use its own current data to enhance its understanding of return behaviour.

Therefore, this investigation will provide value to STADIOalot by determining if its existing data can be utilised to identify orders with increased risk of resulting in a return. Rather than attempting to remove returns completely, the investigation will demonstrate evidence supporting a more proactive strategy for identification of potentially avoidable returns prior to fulfilment costs being incurred. Utilising such a strategy may allow STADIOalot to reduce unnecessary operational expenses, improve data-driven decision-making, and protect profit margins.
