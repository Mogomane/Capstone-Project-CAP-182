# PART B: PROBLEM STATEMENT

## Problem Statement

Although STADIOalot's historical data is rich in detail on all orders placed with them, they have seen an increase in their overall **"order return rate"** since January 2019 from **11 percent to 15 percent**. More specifically, the **"return rate"** for clothing/shoes has risen significantly since January 2019 from **22 percent to 29 percent**. STADIOalot have been able to isolate many factors which contribute to higher than average **"order return rates,"** including products, sellers, size-and-colour combinations and buyer behaviour. These contributing factors are, however, currently not being used by STADIOalot to assist in identifying potentially higher-risk orders prior to receiving confirmation of sale. The inability to proactively address fulfilment costs prior to shipment/fulfilment limits STADIOalot's ability to respond to potentially avoidable returns.

As previously noted, (Minnema et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2022) both consumer-related and product-related characteristics impact the propensity to return. Further, previous studies have explored the use of predictive and machine learning techniques for forecasting product returns (Cui et al., 2020; Mishra & Dutta, 2024; Karl, 2025; Cao et al., 2026). While the literature suggests there is promise in applying predictive modelling and machine learning techniques to historical data, there is no empirical evidence that demonstrates how well these methods will perform on STADIOalot's unique set of data related to all aspects of their customers' purchasing behaviour, including purchases made through STADIOalot.com, transactions completed over phone or at retail locations, the types of items purchased, the behaviours exhibited by each individual customer during their first and subsequent visits to the site, and historical returns information.

Therefore, a very specific operational and analytical challenge exists. Although STADIOalot may be able to find some clues about why customers may return within their history of customer transactions/orders, they lack a statistically significant model that can predictively evaluate the likelihood of potential future returns based upon a customer's past purchasing behaviour.
